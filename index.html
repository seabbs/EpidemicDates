Onset imputation by back-sampling reporting delays

An interactive exploration of potential biases.

The BDBV hub imputes missing onset dates by subtracting a random delay drawn from the pooled empirical delays (issue #61). Are there potential issues with that or similar approaches?

How the methods work

A case is reported on day t with no onset date. Where does each method put its onset? Ink is where it truly falls, given the simulated epidemic. Uses the scenario set below.

Notation: a case reported on day t has unknown onset day s. C(s) is expected confirmed onsets, f(d) the forward onset-to-report delay, F its CDF, π(t, d) the chance an onset is missing, and T the data cut-off.

MethodWhere a missing onset goesIn words
TruthP(s | t) ∝ C(s) f(t−s) π(t, t−s)Depends on how many onsets there were on each earlier day.
HubP(s | t) = g(t−s),   g(d) ∝ f(d) Σu ≤ T−d C(u)(1−π)Pooled delays of complete cases reported by T. The same for every t.
Mean shifts = t − round(Σd d g(d))Every missing case moves back by the same amount.
Forward drawP(s | t) = f(t−s)The delay from onset, ignoring how many onsets there were.
GenerativeP(s | t) ∝ λ(s) f(t−s),   λ(s) = [ncomplete(s) + nallocated(s)] / F(T−s)EM back-projection: share missing cases by the current onset curve, re-estimate the curve, repeat.

Steady growth. If C(s) ∝ ers and π is constant, the truth is ∝ f(d) e−rd. The sum in g is also ∝ e−rd, so the hub draw matches the truth. When growth changes, the two differ.

Delays. f is a double interval censored daily Gamma (primarycensored form): f(d) = G(d+1) − 2G(d) + G(d−1), with G(x) = ∫0x F(u) du.

Rt. From the weekly growth rate r with a Gamma generation interval (shape k, scale θ): R = (1 + rθ)k.

A proper model. Here the generative method is given the true delay, and that is fine for this demonstration. A full model would estimate a time-varying delay jointly with a smoothed onset curve (for example a renewal process) and could model ascertainment, as epinowcast does.

Summary by method

Notes
  • Cases reported on day t need the backward delay, ∝ C(t−d) f(d). The pooled empirical delay matches it only when growth is steady.
  • Generative = EM back-projection, a partial deconvolution. Complete cases keep their onsets. Each missing case reported on day t is shared over onset days s in proportion to λ(s) f(t−s). λ is re-estimated from complete and shared cases, divided by F(T−s) for right truncation, until it settles. This is EM for the likelihood with the missing onsets as latent, as in Becker's HIV back-projection and Richardson–Lucy deconvolution.
  • Here it gets the true delay and expected counts, and assumes onsets are missing at random given report day. That makes it a best case. With noisy counts it needs smoothing or a model (e.g. a renewal process or epinowcast), and the delay has to be estimated. The Python cross-check estimates the delay jointly and stays close to unbiased.
  • Delays are double interval censored daily Gamma pmfs (primarycensored form, uniform primary event), so the daily mean equals the Gamma mean. The hub draws observed whole-day delays, which need no correction. A fitted forward delay would need this correction and a right-truncation adjustment, as in primarycensored or epidist.
  • Growth r(t) is linear between points every 15 days, plus optional mean-reverting noise. Beyond day 240 it holds the last value.
  • Ebola settings: BVD 2026 early r 0.03–0.09/day, Rt about 1.8 falling to 1.0. Onset-to-sample delay mean 7.4 days. Isiro 2012 r about 0.008/day.
  • Expected values. Turn on replicates to compare with noise. Weeks under 80% reported by the cut-off are shaded and excluded from growth and peak metrics.
  • Cross-checked against an independent Python simulation. Source and tests: seabbs/epidemic-imputation-demonstration.
